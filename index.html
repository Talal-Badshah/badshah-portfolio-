Featured Project

Final-year engineering project

Design and Development of a TEG and PCM-Based Hybrid Heat Exchanger Solar Plate

Currently Under Construction / In Progress

This final-year project focuses on the design and development of a hybrid solar thermal system integrating Thermoelectric Generators (TEG) and Phase Change Materials (PCM) with a solar plate / heat-exchanger configuration. The project aims to explore improved utilization of solar thermal energy by combining heat-transfer enhancement, thermal energy storage, and thermoelectric energy conversion within an integrated system.

Thermoelectric Generators can convert a temperature difference directly into electrical energy, while Phase Change Materials can absorb and store thermal energy during phase transition. By integrating these technologies with a solar thermal plate and heat-exchanger system, the project investigates the potential for improved thermal management, temperature regulation, energy recovery, and overall utilization of available solar energy.

Project Goals

  • Design a functional hybrid solar thermal system
  • Integrate TEG technology for thermoelectric energy conversion
  • Incorporate PCM for thermal energy storage and temperature management
  • Improve utilization of solar thermal energy
  • Study heat-transfer behavior and thermal performance
  • Evaluate interaction between thermal storage and thermoelectric generation
  • Develop and test a practical engineering prototype

Overview & Future Work

  • Current stage: concept design and system planning
  • Engineering challenge: combining thermal storage with energy conversion in one unit
  • Future work: prototype completion, experimental testing
  • Thermal-performance analysis and thermoelectric output evaluation
  • Optimization and comparison of system performance
